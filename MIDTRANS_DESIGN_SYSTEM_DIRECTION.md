# MIDTRANS Design System Direction

## Purpose

This document proposes visual design directions for MIDTRANS. It is a design audit output only and does not authorize implementation.

The design system must make MIDTRANS look like:

- An international freight forwarding company
- A logistics and supply chain operator
- A customs and documentation discipline partner
- A company with Syria, UAE, Jebel Ali, China, Turkey, GCC, and Europe corridor experience
- A business with digital logistics and platform capability

## Core Design Requirements

MIDTRANS should visually communicate:

- Operational control
- International movement
- Route intelligence
- Customs and documentation discipline
- Reliability and practical execution
- Corporate seriousness
- Modern digital support without looking like a software-only startup

## Recommended Brand Foundation

### Color System

Primary palette:

- Deep navy: authority, sea freight, corporate logistics
- MIDTRANS blue: freight forwarding, trust, action
- White / soft grey: clarity and operational cleanliness
- Cool steel grey: documents, customs, structure
- Controlled green accent: clearance, verified status, successful steps
- Amber accent used sparingly: warning, document attention, customs risk

Suggested roles:

- Navy for hero backgrounds, headings, footer, important panels
- Blue for buttons, links, active states, route highlights
- Green for verification, shipment milestones, customs-ready status
- Amber only for alerts, document risk, or required action

Avoid:

- Too much neon
- Startup-style gradients everywhere
- Random color tags without logistics meaning
- Generic stock blue with no hierarchy

### Font System

Recommended style:

- Modern sans-serif with strong readability
- Clear heading weight
- Generous line height for operational text
- Distinct small uppercase labels for route/service metadata

Typography should feel:

- Professional
- International
- Operational
- Not decorative
- Not overly playful

### Button Style

Primary buttons:

- Solid MIDTRANS blue
- Rounded but not childish
- Strong contrast
- Clear labels such as `Request a Freight Quote`, `Send Shipment Details`, `Contact MIDTRANS`

Secondary buttons:

- White or light background with blue border
- Used for `Contact`, `Route details`, `Verify MIDTRANS`, `Learn more`

WhatsApp action:

- Green accent but visually controlled
- Should not dominate every page unless urgent contact is primary

### Card Style

Cards should be used as structured logistics modules, not generic boxes.

Recommended card types:

- Service capability card
- Route card
- Document checklist card
- Customs note card
- Milestone card
- Trust signal card
- CTA card
- Related link card

Each card type should have a distinct purpose and visual hierarchy.

### Icon Style

Recommended:

- Line icons or simple solid icons
- Consistent stroke width
- Logistics-specific subjects:
  - ship
  - aircraft
  - truck
  - warehouse
  - document
  - customs stamp
  - route map
  - container
  - dashboard
  - shield/verification

Avoid:

- Random icon libraries mixed together
- Cartoon icons
- Icons that are decorative but do not explain the service

### Image and Graphic Style

MIDTRANS should use a controlled visual language:

- Port and container photography
- Warehouse and cargo handling imagery
- Route map abstractions
- Document workflow visuals
- Platform dashboard mockups where approved
- Customs/document control diagrams

Avoid:

- Generic smiling business people
- Overused global map stock images
- AI robot imagery
- Unrealistic futuristic visuals

## Option A: Corporate Logistics / Premium Freight Forwarder

### Visual Feeling

Serious, established, international, operational, and trustworthy.

Design language:

- Deep navy and white
- Strong typography
- Premium logistics photography
- Structured service grids
- Timeline trust sections
- Route cards with clean metadata
- Conservative but polished

### Best Use Case

Best for company credibility, B2B freight forwarding, customs clearance, and clients who need trust before sending cargo details.

### Pros

- Strong fit for freight forwarding and customs work
- Builds trust quickly
- Low risk of looking exaggerated
- Works well for protected SEO pages
- Supports Damascus 1998 and Dubai/Jebel Ali credibility

### Cons

- May underplay digital platform and AI capabilities
- Can look traditional if not executed with modern spacing and visuals
- Needs high-quality logistics imagery

### Risk Level

Low.

### Fit for MIDTRANS

Strong fit. This is the safest direction for credibility.

## Option B: Digital Logistics Platform / AI-Powered Supply Chain

### Visual Feeling

Modern, technology-driven, platform-based, data-oriented, and fast.

Design language:

- Dark/blue digital gradients
- Dashboard panels
- AI assistant references
- Route network graphics
- Shipment status cards
- Platform CTAs
- Digital workflow sections

### Best Use Case

Best for promoting `midtrans.org`, customer portal, shipment visibility, document dashboards, ERP workflow, and AI freight assistant.

### Pros

- Communicates modern digital capability
- Differentiates MIDTRANS from traditional freight sellers
- Supports platform bridge pages
- Useful for client portal and operational dashboard messaging

### Cons

- Risk of looking like software-only company
- Risk of overclaiming AI if features are not visibly live
- May weaken trust for customs and freight execution if too futuristic
- Could conflict with existing current-site tone

### Risk Level

Medium to high.

### Fit for MIDTRANS

Partial fit. Good for platform pages, not ideal as the main corporate website identity.

## Option C: Hybrid Serious Logistics Company + Modern Digital Platform

### Visual Feeling

Established freight forwarder with modern operational systems.

Design language:

- Corporate navy/blue/white foundation
- Practical logistics photography
- Controlled route and document diagrams
- Digital platform cards used selectively
- Trust and operations first
- AI/digital support presented as an operational layer, not as hype

### Best Use Case

Best for the main MIDTRANS website because it balances freight credibility with digital logistics capability.

### Pros

- Preserves serious logistics identity
- Adds modern platform perception
- Supports services, trade lanes, quote, customs, and platform pages
- Reduces risk of exaggerated AI claims
- Works across protected SEO pages
- Can scale into a full template system

### Cons

- Requires careful design discipline
- Digital elements must remain controlled and factual
- Needs component rules to prevent pages becoming visually inconsistent

### Risk Level

Low to medium.

### Fit for MIDTRANS

Best fit. MIDTRANS should look like a serious logistics operator that uses digital tools, not a tech startup pretending to ship cargo.

## Recommended Direction

Recommended direction: **Option C - Hybrid serious logistics company + modern digital platform**.

Reason:

MIDTRANS business value is operational: freight forwarding, customs coordination, documentation, warehousing, Jebel Ali handling, China/Syria/UAE lanes, and route discipline. The digital platform is important, but it should support this operational credibility rather than replace it visually.

## Design System Priorities

1. Establish premium logistics visual identity.
2. Create reusable hero patterns by page type.
3. Build strong service and trade lane card systems.
4. Add route/document/process visualization.
5. Upgrade CTA blocks.
6. Add trust and milestone components.
7. Improve mobile scanning.
8. Introduce platform visuals only where relevant.

## Recommended Risk Position

Overall redesign risk: Medium.

Reason:

The visual upgrade can be done safely, but many target pages are protected and SEO-important. Redesign should begin as a staging pilot, not a live global change.
