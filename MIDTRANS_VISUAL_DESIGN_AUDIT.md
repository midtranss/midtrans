# MIDTRANS Visual Design Audit

## Scope

This is a design audit only. No live website files, SEO metadata, canonicals, hreflang, sitemap, robots.txt, redirects, header, footer, or protected page content are changed by this document.

Pages reviewed from the current public website structure:

- Homepage `/`
- About Us `/about-us/`
- Services hub `/our-services/`
- Sea Freight service page `/our-services/seafreight/`
- Trade lanes hub `/trade-lanes/`
- Jebel Ali customs clearance `/jebel-ali-customs-clearance/`
- China to Syria route page `/shipping-from-china-to-syria/`
- Dubai to Syria related route coverage via `/land-freight-dubai-to-syria/` and referenced Dubai-to-Syria route structure
- Get Quote `/get-quote/`
- FAQ `/faq/` attempted, but public fetch returned firewall block during audit

## Executive Finding

The current MIDTRANS website has strong operational content but the visual layer does not yet communicate the same level of international logistics authority. The written content presents MIDTRANS as an experienced freight forwarding and customs coordination company, but the design often feels like a functional content site rather than a premium logistics brand.

The main issue is not lack of information. The issue is visual hierarchy, consistency, brand strength, and page presentation. MIDTRANS has enough business depth to look like an integrated logistics and supply chain operator, but the interface does not fully express that capability.

## Current Visual Strengths

- Clear operational language around route review, document readiness, customs exposure, and corridor planning.
- Useful page architecture for services, trade lanes, route pages, quote requests, and FAQ.
- Strong business proof points: Damascus 1998, China coordination, UAE/Jebel Ali capability, FIATA/IATA/WCA/PPL references.
- Good CTA intent: Request Quote, Contact, WhatsApp, Track Shipment, Freight Tools.
- Route pages contain practical logistics details rather than generic SEO filler.

## Current Visual Weaknesses

### 1. Hero Sections

Current hero sections are text-heavy and structurally useful, but not visually premium enough.

Weaknesses:

- Hero areas feel informational rather than branded.
- The homepage hero does not create a strong first impression for an international freight forwarder.
- Route and service page heroes use similar patterns, reducing page identity.
- Visual assets, route graphics, operational diagrams, or logistics imagery are limited or not dominant enough.
- Trust and operational control are mostly communicated through text instead of design.

Impact:

MIDTRANS may look like a small shipping information site instead of a serious freight forwarding and supply chain operator.

### 2. Typography

The typography appears clear but not distinctive enough for a premium logistics brand.

Weaknesses:

- Heading hierarchy does not always create strong visual authority.
- Body copy can feel dense because many pages contain long operational paragraphs.
- Important operational terms do not always receive visual emphasis.
- Repeated text patterns reduce perceived editorial quality.

Impact:

The content is serious, but the reading experience can feel heavy and template-driven.

### 3. Spacing and Section Rhythm

The current site includes many sections, but the spacing rhythm does not always guide the reader clearly.

Weaknesses:

- Long pages can feel stacked instead of intentionally composed.
- Route pages have many useful sections, but visual separation between phases of the shipment journey could be stronger.
- Trade lane lists and filters can feel dense.
- CTA sections do not always feel like premium conversion moments.

Impact:

Users may scan less effectively, and high-value service information may not stand out.

### 4. Color System

The current design appears to rely on a practical blue/white logistics palette, which is appropriate, but it needs stronger systemization.

Weaknesses:

- Blue is suitable but could be more premium with deeper navy, sharper contrast, and controlled accent colors.
- Important labels, tags, buttons, and route modes need a more consistent color logic.
- Visual emphasis is sometimes flat.

Impact:

The brand feels functional but not fully corporate or premium.

### 5. Buttons and CTAs

The site has good CTA intent, but the visual style can be stronger.

Weaknesses:

- Request Quote, Contact, WhatsApp, Track, and platform actions need a clearer hierarchy.
- Primary CTA should feel more decisive and premium.
- Secondary CTAs should be visually distinct without competing.
- Repeated CTA text may feel mechanical if not visually structured.

Impact:

The user sees actions, but the conversion path does not always feel guided.

### 6. Cards

Cards are heavily used across services, trade lanes, internal links, tools, and benefits.

Weaknesses:

- Card patterns can become visually repetitive.
- Trade lane cards need stronger route identity and mode presentation.
- Service cards should look more like logistics capability modules, not basic content boxes.
- Some card groups need better density control on mobile.

Impact:

Cards communicate structure, but not enough differentiation between services, routes, proof points, and CTAs.

### 7. Icons and Visual Language

The current site needs a stronger icon and visual system.

Weaknesses:

- Sea, air, land, customs, warehousing, documents, tracking, and platform concepts should have consistent icon language.
- Icons should not feel generic or decorative only.
- Route pages need visual indicators for origin, destination, transport mode, customs, documents, and delivery scope.

Impact:

The site misses an opportunity to explain complex logistics visually.

### 8. Images and Graphics

The website would benefit from a controlled image/graphic system.

Weaknesses:

- Limited use of premium logistics photography or abstract route graphics.
- Missing strong visual proof of international freight, ports, warehouses, documents, and operations.
- Digital platform positioning is not visually integrated enough.

Impact:

MIDTRANS capabilities are described but not visually demonstrated.

### 9. Trade Lane Presentation

The trade lanes page is strategically important but visually crowded.

Weaknesses:

- Large number of routes creates scanning fatigue.
- Filters are useful but need stronger layout hierarchy.
- Route cards should better separate strategic lanes, regional groups, and specialized freight.
- Mode tags like Sea, Air, Land, LCL, FCL, Customs need more refined visual treatment.
- Duplicate or near-duplicate route listings reduce perceived quality.

Impact:

The trade lane hub has valuable content but risks feeling overloaded.

### 10. Service Presentation

Services are clear, but the visual system should make MIDTRANS look broader and more integrated.

Weaknesses:

- The services hub appears too basic compared with the operational complexity MIDTRANS offers.
- Service pages should show process, documents, route relevance, and related services with stronger visual modules.
- Warehousing, contract logistics, customs, and platform support should feel connected.

Impact:

Users may see separate services instead of an integrated logistics system.

### 11. Trust Signals

Trust content exists but should be elevated visually.

Weaknesses:

- Timeline milestones on About should be more visually authoritative.
- FIATA, IATA, WCA, PPL references need careful visual presentation without overclaiming.
- Operational proof points should appear near CTAs and service explanations.
- Office presence and corridor history should feel like a trust layer, not just text.

Impact:

MIDTRANS has strong credibility, but design does not fully convert it into trust.

### 12. Mobile Layout

Mobile behavior needs special attention because route and service pages are content-heavy.

Weaknesses:

- Dense card lists and filters can become tiring on small screens.
- CTAs should remain visible but not intrusive.
- Long pages need better mobile section anchors or summary blocks.
- Trade lane filters and route cards require simplification.

Impact:

Mobile users may struggle to find the right service or route quickly.

## Overall Design Impression

Current design feeling:

- Somewhat generic: Yes
- Too old: Partially
- Too flat: Yes in important sections
- Too crowded: Yes on trade lanes and long route pages
- Too template-like: Yes in repeated cards and internal link sections
- Too weak for international logistics: Partially
- Not aligned with serious freight forwarding: Content is aligned; visual strength is not fully aligned

## Main Visual Weakness

The website has strong operational content but lacks a premium visual system that turns that content into a confident international logistics brand. The design needs stronger hierarchy, more refined logistics-specific components, better route/service visualization, and a clearer trust/operations layer.

## Recommended Audit Conclusion

MIDTRANS should not simply receive a cosmetic redesign. It needs a controlled design system for logistics pages:

- Premium corporate logistics visual identity
- Structured route cards
- Service capability modules
- Document/control process visuals
- Trust and milestone components
- Digital platform bridge components
- Mobile-first scanning patterns

The strongest direction is a hybrid visual system: serious logistics company first, modern digital platform second.
