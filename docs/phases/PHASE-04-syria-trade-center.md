## Mission

Publish the operational knowledge MIDTRANS has accumulated since 1998 on Syrian trade procedure,
as the content foundation no competitor can replicate.

## Why content starts here

Every other content opportunity in the original roadmap — US trade, Europe, China, industry
verticals — is a subject where MIDTRANS competes with well-resourced publishers on roughly equal
information.

Syrian import and export procedure is not. MIDTRANS has operated through Damascus, Latakia and
Tartous for 27 years. That is an information asset that cannot be bought, researched, or
generated.

If content is going to be written, it starts where the advantage is absolute.

---

## The binding constraint: knowledge capture

The knowledge for this phase lives in the heads of MIDTRANS operations staff, not in documents.

**Structured capture sessions are the critical path.** They must be scheduled and confirmed
*before* the phase starts. If they do not happen, this phase will silently degrade into generic
trade content — which fails the uniqueness test and is worth nothing.

Suggested capture structure per topic:

- What is the actual sequence of steps, in order?
- What documents are genuinely required, and who issues each?
- Where does this most commonly go wrong, and what does it cost the shipper in delay?
- What do first-time importers consistently get wrong?
- What changed in the last few years that published guidance has not caught up with?

The answers to question 3 and question 5 are where the defensible content is.

## Scope

### In scope

- Syria import procedures
- Syria export procedures
- Customs documentation and requirements
- Border and crossing procedures
- Port pages: Latakia, Tartous
- Damascus logistics
- The hub that ties them together, promoted in header and footer

### Out of scope

- Duty rates, tax figures, clearance costs, demurrage figures
- Clearance time estimates
- Sanctions, compliance, or restricted-party determinations
- Any statement that a specific shipment will clear
- Legal advice in any form

### Page budget

**25–40 pages.** Not more.

This is a ceiling, not a target. If the knowledge capture supports 28 genuinely deep pages, the
phase ships 28. Padding to 40 with thin material defeats the purpose of starting here.

## Deliverables

### D1 — Syria Trade & Procedures Center hub

The entry point, linked from the site header and footer.

Structured as a genuine navigational and topical hub — not a link list. It orients a reader who
does not yet know what they need to ask.

### D2 — Import procedures cluster

Pillar: importing into Syria. Clusters beneath it:

- Required documentation, by shipment type
- The clearance sequence, step by step
- Common delays and their causes
- Pre-shipment requirements
- Inspection and compliance processes
- What a first-time importer should prepare before booking

### D3 — Export procedures cluster

Pillar: exporting from Syria. Clusters beneath it:

- Export documentation
- Origin documentation and certification
- The export clearance sequence
- Common rejection causes at origin
- Packaging and marking requirements in practice

### D4 — Documentation reference

The single authoritative location for document requirements. Every other page links here rather
than restating — per `../standards/SEO-STANDARDS.md` §6, procedural facts live in one place so
they cannot contradict each other as procedures change.

- What each document is, who issues it, what it is for
- Which documents apply to which shipment types
- Worked examples of correctly prepared documents
- The errors that cause rejection

### D5 — Ports and border crossings

Latakia, Tartous, Damascus logistics, and the land crossings.

Each page must carry genuine operational substance — the terminal layout as it affects cargo
handling, the practical constraints, the documentation flow specific to that point of entry, what
MIDTRANS has learned operating through it.

**These pages must pass the uniqueness test explicitly.** A port page that reads like an
encyclopedia entry is exactly the thin content this programme exists to avoid. If the operational
detail for a crossing is thin, merge it into a parent page rather than publishing a weak one.

### D6 — FAQ layer

Sourced from Phase 00 / D3 recurring questions and Phase 02 MIRA transcripts. Real questions from
real enquirers — never invented to attach `FAQPage` schema.

### D7 — MIRA knowledge-base integration

Every page in this phase becomes a sourced MIRA knowledge-base entry, with owner and expiry per
`../standards/MIRA-GUARDRAILS.md` §5.

**This is a compounding effect:** the content makes MIRA materially more capable on the subject
that matters most, which raises MIRA's qualified-lead rate, which raises the return on the
content.

## Technical requirements

- Hub, pillar, cluster, detail structure per `../standards/SEO-STANDARDS.md` §3
- Breadcrumbs reflecting the real hierarchy
- `Article`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`, `WebPage` schema, correctly translated
- Arabic is **written**, not translated — see `../standards/WRITING-STANDARDS.md` §6
- Correct RTL, Cairo font, correct bidirectional rendering of Latin terms, port codes and HS codes
  inside Arabic sentences
- Mobile CWV pass; long-form pages must not be heavy
- In-page navigation for long documents
- Printable and shareable — these are documents people send to colleagues

## SEO requirements

- Every page passes the uniqueness test in `../standards/SEO-STANDARDS.md` §2
- Answer-first: the core question answered in the first 100 words
- Self-contained H2 sections that make sense quoted alone
- Consistent entity naming across the entire cluster
- Minimum 3 relevant inbound internal links per page; no orphans
- No contradiction with existing MIDTRANS content — reconcile before publishing

## CRO requirements

- Every page carries the Phase 01 CTA set, contextually labelled
- MIRA accessible throughout
- Relevant tools linked where they genuinely help
- Consultation path offered on procedural pages — a reader working through customs procedure is a
  strong consultation candidate

## Language scope

**T2 — EN + AR.**

Rationale: the audience is Arabic-speaking traders and international importers who read English
professionally. Extending this cluster to seven languages multiplies the work several times over
for readers who are not there. Promotion to a higher tier is available at a later gate if data
shows demand.

## Risks and rollback

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge capture does not happen** | Schedule and confirm before phase start; it is the critical path and the phase's whole premise |
| Content drifts generic to hit the page count | Uniqueness test mandatory per page; 25–40 is a ceiling, and shipping fewer is acceptable |
| Published procedure becomes outdated | Named owner and review date per page; quarterly review cycle; MIRA entries carry `expires_at` |
| A cost or timing figure is published | Explicit out-of-scope list; review checklist item; editorial sign-off |
| Content strays into compliance or legal determination | Out of scope; escalate any such question to a human specialist |
| Arabic delivered as machine translation | Arabic authored, not translated; native domain review required |
| Key-person dependency | Capture sessions recorded and documented, so the knowledge outlives the individual |
| Contradiction with existing site content | Reconciliation pass before publishing; single-source rule for procedural facts |

**Rollback:** content is additive. Individual pages can be unpublished with a 301 to their parent
hub. No existing page is removed or rewritten without explicit approval.
