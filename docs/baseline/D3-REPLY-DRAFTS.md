## نبذة بالعربية

ست مسوّدات جاهزة للإرسال بعد مراجعة فريق العمل. مبنية على **قراءة كل رسالة كاملة** — فلا واحدة منها
تسأل عن معلومة أرسلها العميل فعلاً، وهذا بالضبط الخطأ الذي وثّقه `D3 §5`.

**لا سعر، ولا مدّة، ولا قبول، ولا ضمان مساحة، ولا حكم جمركي** في أيٍّ منها. مُرِّرت كلّها على
`mira/guardrails.py` — صفر اعتراض.

وفي التشغيل الأول حُجبت خمس من سبع — **وكانت الضوابط هي المخطئة لا المسوّدات.** التفصيل في آخر
الملف؛ الخلاصة أن أرقام القوائم المرقّمة أرقامٌ أيضاً، وMIRA تسأل أسئلتها في قائمة مرقّمة.

كل مسوّدة تحتها ثلاثة أسطر: ما أرسله العميل، وما ينقص فعلاً ولماذا، وما يجب **ألّا** يُقال.

قبل الإرسال: املأ `[الاسم]` و`[الرقم]`، وتأكّد أن رقم واتساب هو الصحيح للمكتب المعني.

## كيف كُتبت

Four rules, applied to all six:

1. **Nothing is asked that the enquirer already sent.** Each draft was written after reading the
   full message. Three of the six are more complete than most RFQ wizard submissions.
2. **Every question carries its reason.** "So the pricing desk can approach the carrier" converts
   a demand into a collaboration, and it is the difference between a reply that gets answered and
   one that does not.
3. **No figure, no duration, no acceptance, no capacity, no regulatory determination.** Verified
   mechanically, not by intention.
4. **Short.** A reply that is four days late and also long reads as compensation.

**On the delay:** each draft acknowledges it in one clause and moves on. No paragraph of apology —
that makes the delay the subject of the email instead of the shipment.

## Checked mechanically — and what the check found

Every draft above was run through `mira/guardrails.py`, the same check that screens MIRA's
output before it reaches a customer.

```
7 drafts checked · 0 blocked
```

No rate, no figure in a cost context, no transit time, no hedged figure, no acceptance
commitment, no capacity guarantee. That is not a claim that the drafts are good — it is a claim
about what they do not contain, which is the part a machine can settle.

### The first run blocked five of seven, and the guardrail was wrong

Worth recording, because it is the most useful thing that happened while writing these.

Every one of those five was a **false positive**, and all had the same cause: **ordered-list
markers are numbers.** `normalize()` collapses newlines, so

> `…so we cannot price that line without it. 3. Consignee / importer of record in Syria`

put the digit `3` inside a cost context, and

> `1. approximate number of pallets, and a rough total weight`

put a digit next to a hedge word. Both fired.

This is not a drafting problem. **MIRA asks its qualifying questions as a numbered list** — the
safe fallback in `guardrails.py` does exactly that — so in production this would have suppressed
legitimate clarifying replies and served the fallback instead. `../standards/MIRA-GUARDRAILS.md`
is explicit that a check which blocks legitimate answers gets switched off, and then protects
nothing.

Fixed in three parts, all regression-tested:

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| List markers allowlisted — one or two digits, a period or bracket, then whitespace | Narrow by construction: `4500` and `4.500` do not match, so no rate can hide behind it |
| `hedged_figure` now examines every number in its window, not just the first | Otherwise an allowlisted marker sitting before a real figure would mask it |
| The sentence-end class gained `؟` and `۔` | An Arabic numbered list passed at the first marker and failed at the second, because the preceding character was an Arabic question mark |

Four must-block cases were added alongside, so the allowlist cannot become a way to smuggle a
figure past the check: `1. The rate is USD 4500` still blocks.

**Suite: 82/82.** The drafts were not edited to pass — the check was wrong and the check was
fixed.
